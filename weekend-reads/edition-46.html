The Exam Students Take Twice

In a two-stage exam, students answer alone first and then answer again in small groups. Three days later, they remembered more of what they had discussed.

This week at a glance

The idea  Students take part of the final a second time, together.

Try  A short group stage at the end of your final, worth a small share of the marks.

Time  Twenty minutes of exam time.

Dear Colleagues,

Welcome to the 46th edition of Weekend Reads from CEPI.

Last week we wrote about students who know the material and still struggle to show it on a final. A long question, a tight time limit or a noisy room can get in their way. This letter continues that conversation as finals get closer.

Picture a student in the minute after an exam ends. She walks into the hallway with her classmates, and they start asking each other about question four. For a few minutes they argue about it, and some of them change their answers before they go home.

A two-stage exam brings that talk into the exam room. Students hand in their own papers first. Then they answer some of the same questions again in small groups, sharing one copy.

Most SFBU courses end with a project, a presentation or a portfolio, and a sit-down exam is rare. This letter is for colleagues who still give one when final examinations begin on December 7. Card 3 is for project courses.

  The Two-Stage Exam Study

Dr. Brett Gilley and Dr. Bridgette Clarkston of the University of British Columbia tested two-stage exams with 98 students in a course on natural disasters.[1] Students took each midterm alone first. Then they took it again in groups of three to five, sharing one copy. A few questions were held back from the group stage, and each student answered those a second time alone.

The individual stage was worth 85 percent of the marks and the group stage 15 percent. Three days later, without warning, everyone took a short quiz alone. On questions they had discussed in groups, scores rose about 13 percentage points from the first attempt. On questions they had answered again alone, scores rose about 4 points, so the group stage added about 9 points.

Students gained about the same amount whether they had done well or poorly on the first midterm. The study ran in a three-week summer course and tested memory three days later, so it cannot say how long the gain lasts.

We think the individual stage is what makes the group stage work. Each student commits to an answer alone, and the group stage then tests that answer against someone else’s. Most of each student’s mark still comes from their own paper. For the student who knows the material when she talks, the group stage gives her a place to use it out loud.

 

  Neurodivergent Students and the Group Stage

The review we cited in Edition 45 lists a room without distractions among the exam adjustments that help neurodivergent students.[2] A group stage puts students in a loud room for twenty minutes. For some autistic or anxious students, that may be the hardest part of the exam.

Pairs are quieter than tables of four. A student can also take the group stage alone with their notes, or skip it and have their own paper count for the whole mark. Offer these choices to the whole class, so no one has to explain why they took one. These options are our own suggestions, since the study did not test them.

Students with approved accommodations can work out the right arrangement with Student Accessibility Services.

 

  Three Ways to Try a Two-Stage Exam

After Thanksgiving there is one regular week of classes before December 7, so card 1 is the one most courses can still use.

1  Add a group stage to the final

When as you finish the final, before December 7Time twenty minutes of exam time

Keep most of the final individual, and add a short group stage at the end with a few of the hardest questions. Tell students in your next class, so they know their own answers come first.

Research
In the British Columbia study, the group stage counted for 15 percent of the marks.[1] Students at every level of earlier scores gained about the same.

2  A trial run in the last week

When the week of November 30Time fifteen minutes

If you have a quiz or a review session in the last week, add a short group stage to it. Students see the format once before the final, and you see how your class handles it.

Research
A trial run before the final is our own suggestion.

3  For a project or presentation

When the class before presentationsTime twenty minutes

Each student writes, alone, an answer to the question they most fear hearing about their project. Then the team reads the answers together and agrees on how they would respond.

Research
The study tested exams. Using the same order before a presentation is our own suggestion.
