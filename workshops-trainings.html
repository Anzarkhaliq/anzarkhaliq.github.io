Workshops & Trainings

My workshops are active learning sessions. Participants bring real courses, assignments and institutional problems, and they work on them during the session.

I draw on backward design, Bloom’s Taxonomy, Universal Design for Learning (UDL), TILT, trauma-informed pedagogy, human-centered design and AI integration. Educators and academic leaders leave with artifacts they can use the next day.

Each workshop can run in any of these formats.

Short sessionThree-hour design sprintFull-day workshopTwo-day instituteMulti-day engagement
Course Design and Learning Experience Design
01Cross-Disciplinary Course DesignDetails
In this two-day workshop, participants design a complete course that combines two or more disciplines. They use backward design, Bloom’s Taxonomy, UDL and the TILT framework. Each participant leaves with learning outcomes, assessments, activities and a full course structure.
02Holistic Teaching and Transparent Course DesignDetails
Participants align course goals, assignments, learning activities, student support and communication with students. They study how course design can make learning clearer and more student-centered.
03Modifying Learning Outcomes Using Bloom’s TaxonomyDetails
Faculty revise their learning outcomes for clarity, cognitive complexity, alignment and assessability. They use Bloom’s Taxonomy and backward design. The revised outcomes guide assignment design and match program and institutional outcomes where these apply.
04Evaluating Course Health Using Universal Design for LearningDetails
Faculty review the health of a course with UDL principles. They find barriers to access, engagement and expression. Then they redesign course elements to support a wider range of learners.
Transparent Assessment and AI-Integrated Assignment Design
05Using AI to Create Transparent AssignmentsDetails
Faculty use AI to redesign assignments with the TILT framework. They write the purpose, tasks, criteria, process and expectations in language students can follow. They also test how AI can support feedback and assessment design.
06Creating Custom AI Agents for Everyday Teaching UseDetails
Faculty build custom AI agents for recurring teaching tasks. Examples include rubric creation, in-class activities, formative feedback, course planning and teaching assistant workflows.
07Authentic Assessment in the Age of AIDetails
Participants redesign assessments for a time when AI can produce student work. The new assignments ask for process, reflection, lived experience, context and iteration, so students show their learning directly.
Human-Centered Pedagogy and Student Experience
08Trauma-Informed Practices in EducationDetails
This workshop introduces trauma-informed principles for university teaching. Participants examine how stress, safety, belonging, flexibility and classroom design affect learning and engagement.
09Designing for Student EngagementDetails
Participants design class sessions that increase participation, reflection, dialogue and active learning. Each participant redesigns one session, activity or course component.
10Designing for Student Experience inside Learning Management SystemsDetails
Participants set up Canvas or another LMS so students can use it with little effort. They make critical information easy to find, reduce cognitive load and simplify navigation menus.
Human-Centered Design and Institutional Systems
11Understanding Human-Centered DesignDetails
This is an applied introduction to human-centered design for educators and academic teams. It runs as a three-hour design sprint or as a multi-day workshop on an institutional, curricular or student experience challenge.
12Creating Institutional Frameworks for Teaching ExcellenceDetails
This workshop is for academic administrators. Participants treat faculty hiring, onboarding, evaluation, promotion, recognition, retention and professional development as parts of one teaching system.
