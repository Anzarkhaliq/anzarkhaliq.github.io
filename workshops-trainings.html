Workshops for faculty

Workshops

Active learning experiences built around real courses, real assignments and real institutional challenges.

My sessions and trainings are designed as active learning experiences and not lecture-based trainings. Participants work with real courses, real institutional challenges, real assignments, and real teaching contexts. Across these workshops, I use backward design, Bloom’s Taxonomy, Universal Design for Learning, TILT, trauma-informed pedagogy, human-centered design, and AI integration to help educators and academic leaders build practical artifacts they can use immediately.

Offerings can be adapted for short sessions, three-hour design sprints, full-day workshops, two-day institutes, or multi-day design engagements.

Each workshop can run in any of these formats.

Workshops from Roots & Branches 2026
01Think / Do / CreateDetails
Faculty bring a course they will teach next term and one topic they know well. They write the questions they would ask in class, test them with a partner and keep the ones they can act on. They turn those questions into something students do and something students make, check that the making is authentic, and plan the first week. The session opens with a short talk on the framework. More on the institute.
02The Pyramid After AIDetails
Each faculty member finishes one sentence. It begins: when my students start from an AI output, the thing I most need them to do is… Then each person draws a framework that answers it, in any shape. The frameworks go on a shared wall, the room votes with dots, and every framework joins a shared collection. The session opens with a talk on the Pyramid After AI.
03The Relationship-Rich AssignmentDetails
Faculty interview each other to find one assignment that could include a real person. That person might be a relative, a practitioner, a neighbor or a first client. Each person redesigns the assignment so that it fails without that relationship, tests it with a partner and adds it to a shared collection. The session opens with a talk on relational intelligence.
AI Tools for Faculty, Run as Workshops

Each AI tool I designed at CEPI can run as a hands-on workshop. Faculty bring their own course or assignment, work through the tool’s steps during the session and leave with something they wrote themselves.

04SFBU Course Co-DesignerDetails
A Gemini coach for two faculty members from different fields who are designing one shared course. In six steps they define their students, a real problem both fields touch, a driving question, outcomes, a signature assignment and the place of AI. The pair writes every answer, and the tool turns them into a one-page course outline. It works in English and Spanish.
05SFBU Assignment Pressure-TestDetails
A Gemini coach for testing one assignment. It shows what a chatbot would likely hand in, then checks authenticity and visible reasoning and ranks possible changes. The faculty member writes the revised assignment, and the tool tests it against a chatbot once more.
06CEPI Home Country KitDetails
Faculty type the countries their students come from and one question. The tool builds a printable kit with a page for the instructor and a card for each country. Each card has a QR code that opens the question for that country, so every student starts the discussion from a place they know.
07SFBU Project CoachDetails
A Gemini coach that faculty share with students. A student builds a project from a problem they have seen up close and talks with someone who lives with it. Then the student lists at least five ideas and plans a small test to run in one week. The student writes every answer.
08Designing Authentic AssignmentsDetails
An Alma tool that drafts or revises an assignment in the TILT form, so its purpose, task and criteria are clear from the first draft. Faculty bring an assignment, or a few lines on what students should learn from it.
09Signature Assignment Redesign ToolDetails
An Alma tool for redesigning a course or program signature assignment, so that it asks for the judgment and process AI cannot supply. Faculty bring the current prompt, its rubric and the outcomes it assesses.
10In-Class AI Engagement AssistantDetails
A ChatGPT Edu tool that suggests in-class AI activities, discussion prompts and ways to assess work that involves AI, fitted to a course and topic. Faculty bring the course, the session topic and the class size.
11Setting Up a Course Site on CanvasDetails
An Alma tool that walks faculty through building a Canvas course site step by step, from modules to the home page. Faculty bring their syllabus or course map and a Canvas course shell.
Course Design & Learning Experience Design
12Cross-Disciplinary Course DesignDetails
A two-day workshop where participants design a complete course that combines two or more disciplines. The participants use backward design, Bloom’s Taxonomy, Universal Design for Learning, and TILT framework to move from a course idea to learning outcomes, assessments, activities, and a coherent course structure.
13Holistic Teaching and Transparent Course DesignDetails
A hands-on workshop focused on aligning course goals, assignments, learning activities, student support structures, and transparent communication. Participants examine how course design can create clearer, more meaningful, and more student-centered learning experiences.
14Modifying Learning Outcomes Using Bloom’s TaxonomyDetails
A practical workshop where faculty revise learning outcomes for clarity, cognitive complexity, alignment, and assessability using Bloom’s Taxonomy and backward design principles. Participants refine course outcomes so they better support assignment design, student learning experiences, course coherence, and alignment with program-level and institutional learning outcomes where applicable.
15Evaluating Course Health Using Universal Design for LearningDetails
A diagnostic workshop where faculty review the “health” of a course using Universal Design for Learning principles. Participants identify barriers to access, engagement, and expression, then redesign course elements to support a wider range of learners.
Transparent Assessment & AI-Integrated Assignment Design
16Leveraging AI to Create Transparent AssignmentsDetails
A hands-on workshop where faculty use AI to redesign assignments through the TILT framework. Participants clarify purpose, tasks, criteria, process, and student-facing expectations while exploring how AI can support transparency, feedback, and assessment design.
17Creating Custom AI Agents for Everyday Teaching UseDetails
A practical workshop where faculty build custom AI agents to support recurring teaching tasks, including rubric creation, in-class activities, formative feedback, course planning, and custom teaching assistant workflows.
18Authentic Assessment in the Age of AIDetails
A workshop focused on redesigning assessments in response to AI-generated work. Participants explore how to create assignments that foreground process, reflection, lived experience, context, iteration, and authentic demonstrations of learning.
Human-Centered Pedagogy & Student Experience
19Trauma-Informed Practices in EducationDetails
A workshop introducing trauma-informed principles for higher education teaching. Participants examine how stress, safety, belonging, flexibility, and classroom design affect student learning and engagement.
20Designing for Student EngagementDetails
A workshop focused on creating classroom experiences that increase participation, reflection, dialogue, and active learning. Participants redesign a session, activity, or course component to better support student engagement.
21Designing for Student Experience inside Learning Management SystemsDetails
A practical workshop on designing Canvas or other LMS environments with minimal resistance for students. Participants learn how to make critical information easy to find, reduce cognitive load, improve navigation, and create a more student-friendly digital learning experience.
Human-Centered Design & Institutional Systems
22Understanding Human-Centered DesignDetails
An applied introduction to human-centered design for educators and academic teams. This workshop can be offered as a three-hour design sprint or expanded into multi-day design workshops around custom institutional, curricular, or student experience challenges.
23Creating Institutional Frameworks for Teaching ExcellenceDetails
A workshop for academic administrators focused on building institutional systems that support teaching and learning. Participants examine faculty hiring, onboarding, evaluation, promotion, recognition, retention, and professional development as connected parts of a teaching ecosystem.
