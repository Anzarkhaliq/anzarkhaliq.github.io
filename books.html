My work · 08 of 08 · Essays, letters and a book

How Can We Thrive?

Scientific Inquiry and the Future of Sustainability. The manuscript is under revision and will be published this fall.

Every object in your day reaches you through systems that span the planet. Your phone, your meal and the light above you all depend on them. These systems run so quietly that few of us ever count our share.

In a wealthy country, one person’s share of energy equals about a hundred old-fashioned light bulbs glowing day and night. Most of the water in your life is hidden in your food. The clean car in the driveway carries about the weight of two adults in extra mined metal.

How Can We Thrive? takes you inside these systems: energy, materials, water, food, climate, cities and the living world. At each step I ask what the evidence shows and what the numbers mean. I also name the trade-offs that every honest answer has to face.

The arithmetic never goes past multiplication and a sense of scale. You can rebuild every number yourself. I want you to finish the book able to take apart a weak argument.

I began asking these questions in Karachi in 2014, during an energy crisis. Daily power cuts decided whether hospitals could run and whether children could finish their homework. I came to see energy as a human problem as much as a technical one. More than ten years of teaching it that way became this book.

I wrote the book for any reader who wants to check the numbers behind the headlines about our planet. By the last chapter, I hope you can do that arithmetic yourself and bring better questions to the next claim you hear.

“I want readers to finish this book able to ask better questions of the planet, and of the claims made about it.”
