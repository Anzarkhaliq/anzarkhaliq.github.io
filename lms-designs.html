Designing Where Courses Live

Students and faculty spend much of their week inside Canvas. I design those pages with the same care I give a classroom.

A learning management system is often the first thing a student sees of a course, and it can be the most confusing. Modules pile up, links break and the page that matters sits three clicks deep. I treat the Canvas site as part of the course design. A student should know within a few seconds where they are, what to do this week and why it matters.

The designs on this page are builds and mockups my team and I made at CEPI for SFBU. Each page is written in HTML that runs inside Canvas, and the finished sites are packaged so they can be imported into a course.

Course Templates for MBA Pro+

MBA Pro+ is a set of SFBU business programs built around practice. In each course the student takes on a professional role, such as a strategy consultant advising the executive board of a real organization. Every one of the eight modules adds one deliverable, and by the end the student holds a complete professional package built from their own analysis.

We designed thirteen Canvas page templates for these courses, from the course home page to the page for each deliverable. We also built a full template course to show them in use. Its first module is filled in with a sample course on strategic management and leadership. Every lesson follows the same four phases: build understanding, apply what you have learned, build professional evidence and strengthen future decisions. Students can test an idea at any point with Bayley, SFBU’s AI coach.

The templates went through several rounds. One review showed how far a student scrolled on a phone before reaching anything to click. Every page now opens with a label, a title, a short paragraph, one line of facts and the main button. On a phone, the first button on a module page now sits about 750 pixels from the top, down from more than 2,300. Some decisions are small and honest. A Canvas page cannot know whether a student has finished a step, so the progress track marks only where the student is.

The CEPI Canvas Hub

The Hub is where SFBU faculty find CEPI’s resources. When I audited the old site, it held 87 pages, and 26 of them could not be reached from anywhere. New faculty did not know where to begin.

The rebuild follows the nine sections of the Teaching and Learning Guide. The home page opens with the four PERS qualities and a short path for new faculty: two tools and a conversation with CEPI. Each section page shows where it sits in the guide, how long it takes and what you leave with, followed by examples from four disciplines.

The Hub in the Pro+ Style

I also tested a darker Hub home page in the visual style of the Pro+ courses. This version puts course building first. A faculty member plans the course in a Course Learning Map and builds it from the SFBU course template. Then they check it against CEPI’s course design rubric.

Below that path sit the AI tools CEPI has built for faculty. One helps two faculty design a shared course. Another tests whether an assignment still shows learning when students use AI.

On a Phone

Many faculty and most students open Canvas on a phone, often between classes. Every design here has a phone version, checked at 375 pixels wide. The layout stacks into one column, no text falls below 14 pixels and the main button comes before the pictures.

What Comes Next

The Pro+ templates are being tested in a sandbox course before the first courses are built from them. If you would like to see how these pages are built, or talk about the Canvas sites at your own institution, write to me.
