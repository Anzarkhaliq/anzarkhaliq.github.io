Who I Am

The longer story · CV

I came to education through physics. My doctorate at Sorbonne University took me to synchrotron beamlines, where I spent long nights measuring how materials behave at the scale of atoms. Along the way I hosted a rock and metal radio show and trained in theater and improvisation. Those two worlds kept asking me the same question: what happens to people when they share a room and try to understand something new?

That question brought me to Habib University in Karachi in 2014, as a founding faculty member of the first liberal arts and sciences university in Pakistan. Over ten years I founded its Integrated Sciences and Mathematics program and its Center for Pedagogical Excellence, and co-founded a design lab called The Playground. I also served as its founding Associate Dean for Teaching and Learning.

Today I am the first Chief Learning Officer at San Francisco Bay University, where I lead the Center for Empowerment and Pedagogical Innovation (CEPI). My work sits where three questions meet. How do people learn? How can an institution be designed around that learning? What changes now that AI sits in every classroom?

What I Believe About Learning

All frameworks

Over the past decade I have built a set of frameworks that turn beliefs about learning into decisions a teacher can make on a Tuesday morning. Each one answers a single design question. Together they describe how I think a course should be built.

01

The Pyramid After AI

What should students still do themselves once AI can produce the first draft?

Six human capacities that begin where AI output ends: Decode, Judge, Shape, Author, Transfer and Become.

02

Think / Do / Create

How should one idea travel through a session, a unit or a course?

Students meet an idea, work with it in a real setting, then make something that did not exist before.

03

PERS

What should every learning experience feel like?

Personalized, Experiential, Relationship-Rich and Student-Centered: four qualities that shape course design at SFBU.

04

The New Learning Cycle

What path does one assignment take now that AI is in the room?

Prompt, Generate, Doubt, Argue, Shape, Reflect and Recreate, in place of the old cycle of submit and grade.

How It Reaches Faculty

Roots & Branches · Workshops

Frameworks only matter if teachers can use them. Most of my week therefore goes into faculty development: workshops, one-to-one course redesign and a weekly letter on teaching.

Each August I run Roots & Branches, an annual faculty institute at SFBU. For three days, faculty bring a course they are about to teach and leave with materials they built in the room. In 2026, 32 people took part, including colleagues from Evergreen Valley College and Harvard. The worksheets below are samples of what we work on together.

Teaching now · Fall 2026

I am teaching this course at SFBU in Fall 2026. Before class each week, every student holds a one-on-one conversation with Bayley. Bayley is an AI video companion built with Alma Learning around that week’s sustainability question. It asks each student to take a position, defend it and answer the strongest objection.
