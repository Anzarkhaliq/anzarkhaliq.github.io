Roots & Branches is the annual faculty institute of SFBU. CEPI hosts it, and I direct it. The second edition ran from August 10 to 12, 2026, the week before the fall term began.

Faculty spent three days working on their own courses. Each day asked one design question. Every workshop ended with a course artifact that faculty built in the room and tested with a colleague.

Each workshop opened with a short talk on one idea and the research behind it. Faculty then worked through worksheets, alone and in groups, on a course they teach this term.

Panels, a community conversation and a closing evidence harvest ran between the workshops. I led the Think / Do / Create, Pyramid After AI and relational intelligence sessions. CEPI colleagues and SFBU faculty led the other five workshops.

What Faculty Made

Every participant left with course materials of their own. These included a Think / Do / Create redesign of one unit and a lesson revised with Universal Design for Learning (UDL).

Faculty also wrote a framework for the human capacities their students use alongside AI. They rebuilt an assessment around observable skills and wrote an AI-use statement for Canvas. On the last day, each person redesigned one assignment around relationships and tested it with a peer.

A synthesis of the finished work found the same five patterns across disciplines.

01Purpose before procedurePattern
Students need to know why the work matters, what they are learning and where it connects.
02Ownership increases effortPattern
Choice, lived experience, personal voice and real audiences draw students into the work.
03Reasoning stays visiblePattern
Tasks asked students to explain, compare, justify, critique and choose. These verbs appeared more often than requests to find one answer.
04Learning is iterativePattern
Faculty designed cycles of practice, feedback, revision, testing and a second attempt.
05Transfer is the destinationPattern
Students apply the idea to a new problem, a real use case, a career context or a public product.

Five Practices Faculty Took into the Term

1Name the purpose

State why the work matters.

2Show the process

Ask students to show their thinking along with the product.

3Build a checkpoint

Give feedback before the final deadline.

4Plan another door

Offer prompts, choices and a backup partner.

5Protect authorship

Require judgment, voice and choice.

Reach and Next Steps

Six colleagues joined from outside SFBU. Four came from Evergreen Valley College, one from Harvard and one from a language institute. Evergreen Valley College has since invited CEPI to lead a workshop at its professional development day in January 2027.

Planning for the third edition in 2027 is under way. The plan opens the institute to a larger regional community. It also brings students into the room to co-design and test the materials faculty produce.
