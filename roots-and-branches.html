Why I Built It

Most faculty development on AI takes the form of presentations about change. When I designed Roots & Branches, I wanted faculty to make the change themselves. For three days they work on their own courses in the room and test each piece with a colleague before they leave.

The institute runs the week before the fall term. Each day asks one design question, and every workshop ends with a piece of a real course. I lead the sessions on Think / Do / Create, the Pyramid After AI and relational intelligence. Colleagues from CEPI and SFBU faculty lead the others. The 2026 edition, the second, brought together 32 participants, including colleagues from Evergreen Valley College, Harvard and a language institute.

What Faculty Take Away

1Name the purpose

State why the work matters.

2Show the process

Ask students to show their thinking along with the product.

3Build a checkpoint

Give feedback before the final deadline.

4Plan another door

Offer prompts, choices and a backup partner.

5Protect authorship

Require judgment, voice and choice.

What Comes Next

When we read across everything participants made in 2026, five ideas kept returning in very different disciplines. Students need to know why the work matters. Choice and lived experience increase effort. Reasoning has to stay visible. Learning comes in cycles of feedback and revision, and transfer to a new problem is the destination.

Evergreen Valley College has since invited CEPI to bring the format to its faculty in January 2027. The third edition at SFBU is planned to open to a larger regional community, with students in the room as co-designers. If you would like to run something similar at your institution, I would be glad to talk.
