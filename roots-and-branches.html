Roots & Branches

Why I Built It

Most faculty development on AI consists of presentations about change. When I designed Roots & Branches, I wanted faculty to make the changes themselves. For three days they work on their own courses, and they test each piece with a colleague before they leave.

The institute runs in the week before the fall term. Each day has one design question, and faculty finish every workshop with a piece of a real course. I lead the sessions on Think / Do / Create, the Pyramid After AI and relational intelligence. Colleagues from CEPI and SFBU faculty lead the others. The second edition, in August 2026, had 32 participants, including colleagues from Evergreen Valley College, Harvard and a language institute.

Below are three of the worksheets. Faculty finish each one with something they can use in class the following week.

What Faculty Take Away

1Name the purpose

State why the work matters.

2Show the process

Ask students to show their thinking along with the product.

3Build a checkpoint

Give feedback before the final deadline.

4Plan another door

Offer prompts, choices and a backup partner.

5Protect authorship

Require judgment, voice and choice.

What Comes Next

When we read through everything participants made in 2026, we found the same five ideas in very different disciplines. Students need to know why the work matters. Choice and lived experience increase their effort. Their reasoning has to be visible. They learn in cycles of feedback and revision, and the goal is transfer to a new problem.

Evergreen Valley College has since invited CEPI to run a workshop for its faculty in January 2027. We plan to open the third edition at SFBU to a larger regional community, with students joining as co-designers. If you would like to run something similar at your institution, write to me.

To bring a version of the institute to your campus, write to me at anzar.khaliq@sfbu.edu.
